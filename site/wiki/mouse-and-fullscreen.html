    Mouse and fullscreen

    How the pointer behaves in a streamed app, why one setting matters more than the rest, and how to go fullscreen from Blueprint.

    Two cursors, and only one of them should be visible

    A streamed app has two possible mouse cursors. Unreal can draw one into the frame it renders, and the browser has one of its own. Which you use is the single largest input decision in a Pixel Streaming project.

    SetupWhat it feels like
    Unreal cursor onlyThe cursor lags the hand. It is drawn into the video, so it arrives a round trip late.Avoid
    Browser cursor onlyImmediate. The browser draws it locally and Unreal just receives the input.Use this
    BothTwo cursors on screen, fighting each other.Avoid
    Why the difference is so large

    An Unreal-drawn cursor has to travel to the server, be rendered into a frame, be encoded, and come back before it moves — so every millisecond of network delay is visible in the pointer itself. A browser cursor never leaves the viewer's machine. On a UI-heavy app — a configurator, a menu-driven tool — this is the difference between sluggish and instant.

    If you called Show Mouse Cursor in your Player Controller, you have the slow one. That is the usual way a project ends up with an Unreal-rendered cursor, and it is also the usual reason a stream that is otherwise fast still feels laggy. Turn it off and enable the browser cursor in your app configuration instead.

    Hovering and locked mouse

    Separately from which cursor is drawn, there are two schemes for how the pointer behaves:

    • Hovering — the pointer moves freely over the view, as on a web page. Right for menus, buttons and anything you click on.
    • Locked — the pointer is captured and movement drives the camera, as in a first-person game. Right for looking around.

    Apps that do both usually switch as the viewer moves between them. Place BP_E3DS_PS_Actor in the level and call Change Mouse Control Scheme, choosing HoveringMouse or LockedMouse from the dropdown.

    Without the plugin: add a PixelStreamingInput component to an actor, and send HoveringMouse or LockedMouse as a string with Send Pixel Streaming Response.

    The same switch can be made from the page instead of from the app — see controlling the stream.

    Fullscreen

    With an E3DS_PS_Actor in the level, drag from your trigger's Pressed pin and add the Toggle Fullscreen node. That is the whole Blueprint.

    What you should see

    Pressing the key once puts the browser window into fullscreen; pressing it again leaves. If nothing happens, the viewport did not have focus — click into it first.

    Fullscreen can also be driven from your own page, which is what you want when the button belongs to your interface rather than to the app: fullscreen from the page.
