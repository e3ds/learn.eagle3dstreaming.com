    Docs/System requirements

    Cloud VMs

    For an event, a specific region, or a machine bigger than our standard hardware, your app can run on a dedicated cloud VM — either yours or one we provision for you.

    Start by sending us the app URL

    Whichever route you take, this comes first. From a running app we can measure what it actually needs, rather than guessing from a description of it. That one step lets us tell you whether hardware you already have is enough, what to provision if it is not, and where a choice would cause trouble under load.

    Two routes

    Bring your own VM

    You provision it under your own cloud account. We confirm it meets what your app needs and configure pixel streaming on top. You keep control of the account, the billing and the region.

    Ask us to provide one

    We choose, provision and manage the machine. You send the app URL and, if you have one, a preferred provider. We handle setup and hosting for the event end to end.

    If you are bringing your own, tell us the cloud provider, the GPU model, the VRAM, the CPU tier or core count, and the system memory. Those five let us confirm the machine before you commit to it.

    Do not undersize the VM to save money. Cheapest is rarely the right fit, and underpowered hardware does not fail gently — it stutters, lags, or drops the stream, during the live event you booked it for. Pick from what the app actually needs, which the URL check tells us. If you are weighing a cheaper option, ask us to confirm the minimum viable spec first.

    Hosting a multiplayer server app

    Multiplayer server apps can be hosted free on our shared machines. That is genuinely useful for testing, demos and light multiplayer — and the word doing the work is shared.

    What free shared hosting means

    • Shared infrastructure. Several server apps may run on one machine. A server app that needs substantial CPU, memory or network is not a good fit.
    • United States only. Players far from the US — in Asia or the Middle East, for instance — will feel the distance as latency.
    • Physical machines in our data centre. A power, hardware or network failure there means the server app does not start, and nobody can join a session until it is back.

    For anything with real players depending on it, a dedicated VM from the section above is the answer. The free tier is for proving the thing works, not for running it in front of an audience.
