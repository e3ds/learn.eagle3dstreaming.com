    Streaming app settings · UI

    Fullscreen Button

    Two settings control the fullscreen button your viewers see. What the button can actually do depends on the device, and on phones it differs enough to be worth knowing before you switch it on.

    The two settings

    Fullscreen Button

    Shows the fullscreen button in the stream's control bar. On by default. With it off, no viewer sees the button on any device.

    Even when it is on, the button only appears where fullscreen can actually work. That is the point of the second setting.

    Show it where only video fullscreen is possible

    A child of the setting above, off by default. It makes the button appear on devices that cannot put the page into fullscreen but can put the video into fullscreen — in practice, iPhones.

    Turning it on changes what a viewer can do, not just what they can see. Read the next section before enabling it.

    What happens on each platform

    Device Button shown by default What fullscreen does Can the viewer still use your app?
    Windows / Mac desktop
    Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari    		 Yes The whole stream area fills the screen, with your controls still on top of it. Yes — everything works normally
    Android phone / tablet Yes Same as desktop — the page goes fullscreen. Yes
    iPad Yes Same as desktop. Yes
    iPhone No Only with the second setting on. The video fills the screen in Apple's own video player — not your page. No — see below

    Why the iPhone is different, and why it is off by default

    iPhone Safari does not implement page fullscreen at all. The only thing that can go fullscreen is the video itself, and iOS hands it to the system video player.

    In that mode the system player receives the taps, not your app. The viewer sees your stream filling the screen, and cannot click, drag or interact with anything in it until they leave fullscreen. Your control bar is not visible either.

    That is right for a stream people only watch — a showroom, a walkthrough, a video wall. It is wrong for anything a viewer configures, clicks or plays. That is why it is a choice per app rather than something switched on for everyone.

    Deciding

    • Leave it off if viewers interact with your app — configurators, games, anything with buttons.
    • Turn it on if the stream is watched rather than used, and filling an iPhone screen is worth losing interaction for.
    • Either way, nothing changes on desktop, Android or iPad. This setting only affects devices where page fullscreen does not exist.

    Changes reach viewers the next time they open the stream. Anyone already watching keeps what they started with until they reload.

    If your stream is embedded in another page

    When the stream runs inside an <iframe> on your own website, fullscreen has to be permitted by the page doing the embedding. If it is not, the browser blocks fullscreen entirely and the button is hidden — on every device, including desktop.

    The embedding page needs the permission on the iframe:

    <iframe src="…" allow="fullscreen"></iframe>

    The second setting does not override this, and deliberately so. A blocked iframe means fullscreen cannot work at all — not even video fullscreen — so forcing the button to appear would only give viewers something that does nothing when tapped.

    This section is provisional

    One case has not been tested yet: whether an iPhone can still put the video fullscreen inside an iframe that forbids page fullscreen. If it can, the button could reasonably appear there too. Until that is confirmed, it stays hidden — hiding a button that might work is a smaller mistake than showing one that cannot.

    This page will be updated once that has been tested.
