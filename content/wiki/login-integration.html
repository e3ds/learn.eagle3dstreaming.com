Put a login in front of your stream, so only the people you choose can reach it. The pattern is the same for the SDK and the iframe: authenticate first, then load the stream page.

What follows is a demonstration of the flow, not an authentication system. It shows where the check goes, not how to check securely. A production login belongs on your server — real sessions, tokens or secure cookies — because anything decided in the browser can be changed in the browser.

The flow

  1. The visitor lands on a login page

    Your page, your design, entirely separate from the stream.

  2. Credentials are checked

    On your server. This is the step the demo simplifies and the step that actually matters.

  3. On success, they go to the page holding the stream

  4. That page verifies authentication before loading the stream

    This check is the point of the whole arrangement. Without it, anyone who knows the stream page's address skips the login entirely — and a login you can walk around is decoration.

  5. If the check fails, back to the login page

Where the stream fits

Either integration works. The difference is only what the protected page contains once the visitor reaches it: the SDK's e3ds_controller.main() call, or the iframe with your streaming URL.

Protect the token, not just the page. With the SDK, the session token is what actually grants access to the stream — so request it from your server after the login has passed, rather than embedding a key in a page and relying on the page being hard to find. Embedding with the SDK covers keeping the key server-side.
