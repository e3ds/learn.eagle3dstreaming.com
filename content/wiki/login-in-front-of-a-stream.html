Put a login in front of your stream, so only the people you choose can reach it. The pattern is the same for the Web SDK and the iframe: authenticate first, then load the stream page.

Two checks, and the second is the one people leave out

A login page is the obvious half. The half that actually does the work is the page holding the stream verifying authentication before it loads anything.

Without it, anyone who knows that page's address skips the login entirely — and a login you can walk around is decoration.

The flow

  1. The visitor lands on a login page. Your page, your design, entirely separate from the stream.
  2. Credentials are checked on your server. Not in the browser. Anything decided in the browser can be changed in the browser.
  3. On success they go to the page holding the stream.
  4. That page verifies authentication before loading the stream — the check above.
  5. If the check fails, back to the login page.

Where the stream fits

Either integration works, and the difference is only what the protected page contains once the visitor reaches it: the SDK's e3ds_controller.main() call, or the iframe with your streaming URL.

Protect the token, not just the page

With the Web SDK the session token is what actually grants access to the stream. So request it from your server after the login has passed — rather than embedding a key in a page and relying on the page being hard to find.

This is the difference between a login that gates a page and one that gates a session. Only the second survives somebody reading your page source.

Why this is the SDK's argument, not a feature you configure

With a streaming URL or an iframe, the link itself is what grants access. You can put a password in front of it, but you cannot revoke it for one person, and the URL inside your page is a working link to anyone who reads the source.

With the SDK, your backend decides who gets a token, when, and for which app. That is a different kind of control, and it is the one thing the other two methods cannot be configured into doing. API keys and session tokens covers what each credential is and how long it lives.

What a demo will not show you

Any sample of this flow is a demonstration of where the check goes, not of how to check securely. A production login belongs on your server — real sessions, tokens or secure cookies. Treat the sample as the shape, and your own authentication as the substance.
