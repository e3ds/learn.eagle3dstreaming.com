The trial is usage-based, not time-based. It does not expire on a date — it ends when the 60 minutes are gone. An account left untouched for months still has its trial minutes waiting.

How usage is counted

Minutes and storage are both tracked live and visible in the Control Panel. Minutes are streaming time — time a session is running, whether or not anyone is looking at it.

This is why the idle timeout matters

A session left open in a forgotten tab consumes minutes at exactly the same rate as one being used. Setting logout after inactivity is the single most effective thing you can do about an unexpectedly large bill, and it costs a real viewer nothing.

Capping what you spend

On a prepaid-minute plan, Minutes Streamed Limit puts a ceiling on usage. Enable it under Subscriptions and choose the number of minutes.

The streaming cap is post-paid. You are charged at the end of the month for what was used, so the cap limits how far usage can go — it does not require money up front, and it does not stop a charge for what has already been streamed.

Storage

Accounts start with 10 GB, and up to 500 GB can be purchased from the Control Panel under Subscriptions.

Storage is consumed by uploaded builds — including old versions, if version control is on. Deleting versions you will not roll back to is usually cheaper than buying more space, and it is done from the app.

Changing plan, and stopping

  • Upgrading is done from the Control Panel, and takes effect for the current cycle.
  • Payment details are edited under Subscriptions.
  • Transactions lists what you have been charged — the place to look before asking about a bill.
  • Cancelling stops the automatic renewal. The Core plan renews on its own until you do.

Enterprise is not only a larger allocation. It is the only plan with global deployment, so if your audience is outside the USA and Europe the difference is latency for every viewer, not just a bigger number of minutes. Regions covers what that means in practice.
