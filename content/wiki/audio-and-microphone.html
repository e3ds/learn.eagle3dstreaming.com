Two audio behaviours surprise people: every stream starts muted, and turning on the microphone can stop a stream from starting at all. Both come from the browser, not from your app.

Why the stream starts muted

Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all refuse to play sound on a page the visitor has not interacted with. Video may start on its own; audio may not. Pixel Streaming runs in a browser, so it lives under that rule like any other page.

It fixes itself on the first click

Any genuine interaction unmutes it — the play button, a click inside the streaming area, or a control in your own interface. Nothing to configure, and no change to your Unreal project. This is also why the play button exists rather than being something we could remove.

The audio was never missing. Your app has been producing it all along; the browser was simply not letting it out. Anyone reporting "no sound" who has not yet clicked anything is seeing this and nothing else.

When the microphone stops the stream

If Microphone is enabled in your app configuration, the browser asks the viewer for permission when the page loads. Until that is answered, there is no microphone to attach — and the session waits.

Four situations produce that, and only the first is a deliberate choice:

  • The viewer clicked Block on the permission prompt.
  • The viewer ignored the prompt and carried on waiting.
  • The browser is set to block permission pop-ups, so no prompt ever appeared.
  • Microphone access is disabled in the browser's settings for that site.

The failure does not name the microphone. The viewer sees a stream that would not start, several minutes after the fact, with nothing pointing at a permission they dismissed. If a stream hangs at start-up and the microphone is enabled, check this before anything else.

Fixing it in the browser

  1. On the streaming page, click the lock or settings icon beside the address bar.

  2. Open Site settings.

  3. Set Microphone to Allow, and make sure pop-ups and redirects are not blocked — that is what suppresses the prompt in the first place.

  4. Reload the stream.

If your app does not use the microphone, turn the setting off

An app with the microphone enabled that never listens to it gains nothing and inherits a whole class of start-up failure — one that depends on the viewer's browser settings, which you cannot see or control. Leave it off unless something in your app actually reads audio input.
