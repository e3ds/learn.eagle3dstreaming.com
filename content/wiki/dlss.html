DLSS renders your app at a lower resolution and upscales it with NVIDIA's model. You get frame rate back on a GPU you cannot make faster.

Why it matters more here than on a desktop

On a streamed app the GPU is fixed — it is the machine we allocate, not one the viewer can upgrade. If your project is short of frame rate, you cannot ask for better hardware; you have to render more cheaply. DLSS does exactly that: fewer pixels rendered, the missing ones reconstructed.

And frame rate is what the viewer sees

Every frame your app fails to produce is a frame the encoder cannot send. Rendering headroom shows up directly as smoothness in the stream, which is not true of a desktop build where the display is local.

Adding it to your project

  1. Download the DLSS plugin from NVIDIA, matching your Unreal version.

  2. Copy the DLSS folder into your project's Plugins directory.

  3. Copy the widget plugin too, from plugin → samples → Plugins. This is the on-screen overlay that lets you see and change DLSS settings while running.

  4. Clone the Blueprint folder from the sample content into your project.

  5. Enable Pixel Streaming and DLSS (NVIDIA) under Edit → Plugins, then restart the editor.

  6. Show the widget from your Level Blueprint, so you can toggle DLSS modes at runtime.

Testing it

Launch in Standalone Game mode and confirm the widget appears and responds. Press H to hide the overlay once you have what you need.

Compare frame rates with the overlay, not by eye. DLSS trades a small amount of image reconstruction for a large amount of GPU time, and the point is to find the quality mode where the trade is worth it for your content. Set that mode as your default before packaging — the widget is a development tool, not something to ship to viewers.
