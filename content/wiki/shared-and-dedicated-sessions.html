Two ways several people can use one application: each in their own session, or all watching the same one. They cost differently, and they are for different situations.

Dedicated: everyone has their own

Everyone opens the same URL and each gets an independent session on its own machine — their own view, their own input, their own progress through the app. Nobody affects anybody else.

This is the default, and it is what you want when people are using the app rather than being shown it: a configurator where each customer builds their own product, a training scenario each person works through at their own pace.

Each session is a concurrent user. Twenty people in dedicated mode is twenty machines and twenty CCU. If they are all watching the same thing anyway, shared mode does the same job for one — see capacity.

Shared: everybody watches one

Built on the meeting system. A host starts the session with a host URL and shares a guest URL; everyone who joins sees the host's view of the same running application.

Guests watch by default. They cannot drive the app unless the host explicitly hands them control during the meeting — which is what makes this safe for a presentation: nobody can move the camera while you are mid-sentence.

This suits a demo to a room, a design review where one person drives, or a lesson. Everyone sees the same thing at the same moment, because it is literally the same session.

Choosing between them

Ask whether people need to do different things. If yes, dedicated. If they are all looking at the same thing while one person drives, shared — one machine instead of twenty, and everyone genuinely in step.
