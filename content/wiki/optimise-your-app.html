What to change in your Unreal project to make it stream well: fewer dropped frames, less input lag, and a shorter wait before anyone can use it.

Streaming cannot be faster than your app. Run the packaged build on your own machine and time it — that is the ceiling. The platform is built to add as little as possible on top, but it cannot make a slow application fast. If the native build takes forty seconds to reach its main menu, so will the stream.

The three with the largest effect

Change these first. Together they cost almost nothing and are worth more than everything below.

  1. Stream at 1280 × 720, 30 fps

    Every frame is encoded, sent and decoded. Halving the pixels halves that work at every stage — on our GPU, on the network, and on your user's phone. This single setting moves more than any graphics option.

  2. Turn off motion blur

    It is expensive exactly when you can least afford it: during fast motion, when the encoder is already working hardest. Removing it steadies the frame rate at the moments users notice most.

  3. Do not enable ray tracing

    It adds real-time lighting work to every frame and raises latency directly. It also has its own memory pool, separate from textures, which is a common cause of running out of GPU memory mid-session.

Lighting and graphics

  • Prefer baked lighting. It is computed once, ahead of time. Dynamic lights are recomputed every frame, on both CPU and GPU. Keep the number of dynamic sources as low as the scene allows.
  • Enable the hardware video decoder in Project Settings. It moves decode work off the CPU and onto dedicated GPU hardware, so it stops competing with your application.
  • Trim post-processing. Screen-space reflections, ambient occlusion, bloom and depth of field are applied to every frame, and their cost grows with resolution. Reducing their quality is usually invisible in a stream and measurably cheaper.

The cursor

Two cursors are visible at once unless you prevent it — the browser's and your app's — and the effect is disorienting in a way users report as "laggy" even when the frame rate is fine.

  • Set a custom mouse cursor image with zero alpha, so the in-game cursor is invisible and only the browser's remains.
  • In Project Settings, set the Cursor HUD to software cursors and configure the Pixel Streamer default cursor class.

Load time

The wait before anyone can interact is the part users judge hardest, because it happens before they have seen anything worth waiting for.

  • Stream your levels. Load parts of the map as they are needed rather than all of it at once. The first thing a user sees arrives sooner, and memory stays lower throughout.
  • Show a loading screen. A blank frame reads as a broken stream. You can set one, including a short video, from the Control Panel.

Everything else, in order of what it buys you

AreaWhat to doWhy it helps
Network Wired where possible, and pick the server region closest to your users. Streaming is far more sensitive to jitter and round-trip time than to raw bandwidth.
Bitrate Tune target and maximum bitrate to the audience — lower for mobile and cellular. Trades image quality against buffering, packet loss and dropped frames.
Texture pool Size the texture streaming pool deliberately and use compressed formats. The main lever on GPU memory, and what prevents textures popping in.
Level of detail Configure LODs and distance culling for meshes and foliage. Fewer polygons per frame, directly.
Occlusion culling Enable it, so hidden objects are not drawn. Removes draw calls that could never have been seen.
Scalability Use Unreal's scalability settings for shadows, effects and view distance. One dial for the quality-versus-performance trade, rather than dozens.
Idle timeout Set session and idle timeouts in the Control Panel. Releases machines nobody is using — availability for others, and cost for you.
Browser Recommend an up-to-date Chromium browser: Chrome or Edge. The most consistent WebRTC behaviour, which is what the stream runs on.

On phones

A phone is not a small desktop. It has less bandwidth, a smaller screen, and a thermal budget that a sustained stream will find.

  • Lower the resolution and bitrate for mobile sessions. The screen cannot show the difference, and the connection often cannot carry it.
  • Simplify the interface. Controls sized for a mouse are hard to hit with a thumb, and a stream cannot make a small target easier.
