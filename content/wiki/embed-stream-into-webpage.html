Put your streamed application inside your own website, and let your page and your app talk to each other. There are two ways to do it, and the choice matters more than anything else on this page.

The old SDK no longer works. Our streaming infrastructure was upgraded and the previous SDK is not compatible with it — integrations still using it have stopped streaming, not degraded. If you have one, moving to the current SDK is not optional and not a modernisation; it is the difference between working and not.

Nothing changes in your Unreal application. The work is entirely on the web side.

The Frontend SDK

Your page controls the stream directly. The SDK handles token generation, authentication, the connection and stream setup, so you are not writing low-level streaming code. Use this unless you have a reason not to.

An iframe

The stream runs in its own frame and you talk to it with messages. Simpler to drop in, and isolated from your page's own JavaScript — which is an advantage when the host page is a CMS or something you do not fully control.

Both support the same things: sending data to your app, receiving data from it, controlling quality and resolution, fullscreen, and custom screens for loading and session-end. The pages that follow cover each task both ways, so you do not need to have chosen before reading them.

What you can do once it is embedded

  • Send data to your Unreal app from the page — a button that changes a material, a form that positions a camera.
  • Receive data from the app in the page — a click inside the 3D scene updating your own interface.
  • Control the stream: quality, resolution, fullscreen, mouse behaviour, screenshots.
  • Replace our screens with yours for loading, session expiry and crashes, so a failure looks like your product rather than ours.
  • Whitelist your domain, which removes our branding and restricts where the stream may be embedded.
