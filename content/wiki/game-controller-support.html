PlayStation, Xbox and standard gamepads work in a streamed app with no setup on our side. Connect the controller, open the stream, play.

The browser detects the controller and forwards its input to your app the same way it forwards mouse and keyboard. Nothing needs enabling in your app configuration, and nothing needs a plugin.

The whole procedure

Connect the controller to your device by USB or Bluetooth, open the streaming URL, and use it. That is all — there is no pairing step, no permission prompt, and no configuration.

The one thing that breaks it

Controller support comes from your Unreal project's input mappings, not from the platform. Every Epic template — Third Person included — ships with those mappings already set up, so a template-based project works immediately.

If controller input does nothing, the mappings are missing from the project. That happens with custom projects, third-party kits, and projects built from a non-Epic template, or when gamepad bindings were removed at some point. The fix is in Unreal's input settings, not on the platform: restore the gamepad bindings and repackage.

Checking a controller

If you are unsure whether the controller itself is being seen, test it in the browser before blaming the stream. Any browser gamepad tester will show which buttons and axes register — if they do not appear there, the problem is between the controller and the browser, and your app is not involved.
