The stages an upload and a streaming session pass through, what each one is doing, and what it means when one of them stalls. This is the page to open when something is taking too long.

Uploading an app: six phases

The Control Panel colours each phase: green passed, yellow a warning you can proceed past, red an error you cannot.

PhaseWhat it doesIf it stops here
1. Application review Checks the ZIP holds a packaged Unreal app with an executable, and that Pixel Streaming is enabled. Also scans for viruses. Yellow: plugin not detected — you may continue, and it will not stream. Red: no Unreal app in the ZIP.
2. Upload Transfers the file. A slow or interrupted connection. Press Retry, which restarts from phase 1.
3. Acquiring Begins at 50% uploaded; stores the ZIP on a machine. Usually a ZIP missing files, or the connection dropping. Server problems here are rare.
4. Preparing Extracts and installs the build. Same causes as phase 3 — an incomplete ZIP is the usual one.
5. In queue Waiting, if several apps are being processed at once. Nothing is wrong. A long queue simply takes longer.
6. Testing Runs your app on a machine to confirm it streams. The app itself — a crash on start, or resources beyond the hardware budget.

Check your Pixel Streaming plugin version before uploading. Plugin 2 streams only on Unreal 5.7 and newer, and never supports communication between the page and your app. On Unreal 5.5 or 5.6 it does not stream at all — and the failure surfaces here, at upload testing, rather than when you enabled it. The full compatibility table.

A yellow warning at phase 1 is the one worth stopping for. You are allowed past it, and an app uploaded without Pixel Streaming enabled will never stream. If you see that warning, go back and enable the plugin rather than continuing.

Starting a session: eight phases

PhaseWhat is happeningIf it stops here
1. Opening the URLThe page loads.—
2. Requesting a slotAsking for capacity.See "stuck in the queue" below.
3. Waiting for a machineA GPU machine is being allocated.For a Linux app, check the platform setting — a config set to Windows waits here forever.
4. AcquiringDownloading your app to that machine. Skipped if it is already there.Large builds take longer the first time.
5. PreparingExtracting and setting up. Also skipped if already present.—
6. LaunchingYour app starting.The app's own start-up time — usually the largest share.
7. Press playWaiting for the viewer's click.Not a fault. Browsers require one interaction before video.
8. StreamingVideo is flowing.—

Phases 4 and 5 disappear once a machine already holds your app, which is why the same app can take 25 seconds one time and 12 the next with nothing changed. It is also what preallocation removes permanently.

Stuck in the queue

A short queue when many people start at once is normal and resolves itself. Consistently queuing is not: it means no machine matching your configuration is free.

Check these, in this order

The platform setting if it is a Linux build; then your plan's concurrent-user limit, since every active session holds a slot; then whether sessions are being left open, which an idle timeout fixes.
