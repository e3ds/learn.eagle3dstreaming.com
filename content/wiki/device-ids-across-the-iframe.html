Letting someone choose which microphone a stream uses takes two commands, not one. This page explains why, in order, from the beginning.

1. There are two pages, not one

When you embed a stream, your visitor is looking at two web pages at once. Yours, and ours inside it.

2. The computer has two microphones

Say the visitor's computer has two: microphone 1 and microphone 2. Both pages can see both of them. Nothing surprising yet.

3. But each page calls them by different names

Every microphone has an id — that is how a page says use this one. The catch is that a browser gives each page its own ids for the very same microphones.

4. So your ids are no use to us

This is the whole problem. If your page looks up its own microphones and sends us ID1, we have never heard of ID1. We know ID3 and ID4. The message arrives and means nothing.

5. So ask us for our list first

Send getMicDevices. We reply with our ids — the ones we will recognise when you send one back.

6. Pick one, and send that id back

How you choose is entirely yours. A dropdown, a settings screen, or nothing on screen at all — your code can simply pick one. Whichever it is, send us back the id we gave you.

7. We take it from there

Once we have the id, that is the end of your part. We capture that microphone and send its audio over WebRTC to your Unreal application — without restarting the stream. The picture does not flicker and the session is not re-queued.

The four messages, together

DirectionMessage
your page → us{ cmd: "getMicDevices" }
us → your page{ type: "micDevices", ok: true, devices: [{ deviceId, label, isActive }] }
your page → us{ cmd: "setMicDevice", deviceId: "<one of ours>" }
us → your page{ type: "micDeviceChanged", ok: true, deviceId, label }
us → your page{ type: "micDeviceChanged", ok: false, error, message }

All four are ordinary postMessage traffic across the iframe boundary, the same as every other command.

micDevices can also arrive without being asked for, when a microphone is plugged in or unplugged. Treat it as "here is the current list" rather than only as a reply.

Why not match on the name instead of the id? The label is the same text on both sides, so it looks like a shortcut. It is not a safe one: labels are empty until the microphone has been allowed, two identical devices produce two identical names, and the wording changes with the computer's language. The id is the only dependable handle.

If the list comes back empty, the microphone has not been allowed for our page — which is a separate thing from allowing it for yours. See Choose the microphone under When the permission is the problem.
