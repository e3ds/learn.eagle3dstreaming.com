Your application runs on our servers, not on your users' machines. This is the hardware it gets, the budget it has to fit inside, and what happens if it does not.

What your app runs on

The right-hand column is the one that matters. The machine's full capacity is not yours to spend: the operating system, the encoder that turns your frames into video, and the streaming stack all need room. An app that fits the middle column but not the right one will look fine in testing and fail under load.

The number to check first

GPU memory. Running out of it is the most common reason an upload fails its test or a stream dies mid-session, and the error it produces — Out of Video Memory — usually arrives without warning after several minutes of apparently normal running.

Where to start

These three settings do more for streaming performance than anything else, and cost the least to change:

Recommended baseline

  • 1280 × 720 resolution. Streaming is video, and a smaller frame is a smaller, faster, more reliable video.
  • 30 frames per second.
  • PAK files disabled in Project Settings. They slow the start of every session.

Optimising your app goes considerably further, including the settings that matter specifically on phones.

If your app needs more

Some applications genuinely cannot fit — a large architectural scene, a simulation with heavy physics, anything built for a workstation. Contact support rather than trying to squeeze it in. Custom hardware is available, and a machine sized for the app costs far less than a stream that fails in front of your customers.

An app that exceeds the hardware does not fail politely. It may crash during the upload test, or run for several minutes and then stop mid-session. Neither failure names the resource that ran out, which is why the budget above is worth checking before you upload rather than after.
