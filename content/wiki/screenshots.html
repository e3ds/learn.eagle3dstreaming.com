Capture a frame at Unreal's quality rather than the stream's, and hand it to the viewer as a download.

Why not just screenshot the page

What the viewer sees is compressed video, sized to their window and degraded by their connection. Grabbing that gives you a picture of a video stream. This node instead captures Unreal's own output, before it is encoded — so the result does not depend on the viewer's bandwidth, resolution or connection quality at all.

The difference is not subtle

On a poor connection a browser-side capture is visibly blurry and a plugin-side capture is not. If the image is going into a quote, a configuration sheet or anything a customer keeps, this is the one to use.

The Blueprint

  1. Put an E3DS_PS_Actor in the level you are streaming.

  2. Call CaptureScreenshot from wherever the capture should happen — a Level Blueprint key press, a button in your UI, the end of an animation.

That is the whole of it. The plugin sends the request, the platform captures the frame, and the image downloads in the viewer's browser.

Without the plugin

The node is a wrapper around a message, so you can send that message yourself. Add a PixelStreamingInput component to an actor in the level and send this with Send Pixel Streaming Response: 

{"cmd":"captureScreenShot"}

Only do it this way if you have a reason to. The manual route has the same effect and several more places to get it wrong — a mistyped key, the component on an actor that is not in the level, the response node on the wrong graph. The node exists to remove exactly those mistakes.

Other ways to trigger it

A screenshot can also be requested from the page rather than the app, which is what you want when the button lives in your own interface rather than in Unreal. See controlling the stream.
