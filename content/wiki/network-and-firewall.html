Corporate networks, school networks and public Wi-Fi often block the connections a stream needs. When that happens your app is fine — the network between it and your user is not.

What a blocked network looks like

The stream does not start, or starts and immediately stops. It usually works for you and fails for one customer, or works everywhere except one office — which is the pattern that identifies it. A fault in your app does not care which building the viewer is in.

Three things cause it, in rough order of likelihood:

  • Firewall rules blocking the ports or protocols streaming needs. Corporate filters such as Cisco Umbrella do this by policy, not by accident.
  • A proxy or VPN in the path. Both interfere with the direct connection a stream depends on.
  • Port forwarding or network configuration on the viewer's side that prevents the connection completing.

This is not something we can fix from our side. The block is inside the viewer's network, applied by their organisation, and it is working as their administrator configured it. What we can do is tell you exactly what to ask for.

What to do

  1. Confirm it is the network, not the app

    Open the same stream from a different network — a phone on mobile data is the quickest test, since it shares nothing with the office network. If it works there, the app is fine and the network is the problem.

  2. Contact the IT department or network administrator

    They can say why it is blocked, and whether the restriction is deliberate. Often it is a general category rule that was never aimed at you.

  3. Ask for the streaming URL to be whitelisted

    A specific, small request is far more likely to be granted than "please allow streaming". Give them the exact app URL, and ask that the ports and protocols pixel streaming needs are not blocked for it.

If you are preparing for an event

Test from the venue's network, on the venue's Wi-Fi, before the day. A corporate guest network discovered an hour before a demo is not a problem anyone can solve in an hour.
