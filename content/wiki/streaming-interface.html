The controls a viewer sees on top of your stream: the settings bar, quality readout, screenshots, notifications and voice chat. All of them are off or on per configuration.

Stream link › Edit Config › Advanced Options › Streaming UI. Each is a toggle; save afterwards, and the change applies to new sessions.

The settings bar, and what depends on it

Several settings only appear if the settings bar is on. Settings Control Bar is what puts the gear icon on screen during a session — and the quality menu, the screenshot button and the draggable-button option all live behind that icon. Enable them with the bar switched off and nothing visible changes, which reads as a setting that does not work.

SettingWhat the viewer gets
Settings control bar The gear icon during the session. The gateway to everything below it.
Quality control menu Stream quality readings, useful when diagnosing a poor connection.
Screenshot A capture button in the settings panel.
Draggable settings button Lets the viewer move the gear icon, for when it covers something in your app.
Runtime notifications Messages during the session — warnings and status.

Input

  • Fake mouse with touches turns touches on a phone or tablet into mouse events. Without it, an app built for a mouse receives nothing usable from a touchscreen — and it looks like the stream is ignoring the viewer rather than like a missing setting.
  • Enable browser microphone lets the viewer speak into your app. Microphone settings covers what each related option does and what each end of its range means.

Autoplay

Autoplay starts the video as soon as it is ready, rather than waiting.

It does not remove the first click. Browsers will not start audio and video without one interaction from the user — that is a browser rule and no setting overrides it. Autoplay removes a second, separate wait; the viewer still touches the loading screen once.

Voice and sharing

  • VoIP gives viewers real-time voice communication with each other during a session — for a shared review or a guided demo, rather than for talking to the app.
  • Sharing a session with guests lets other people join the same running session, so a colleague or client sees what you see rather than starting their own.
