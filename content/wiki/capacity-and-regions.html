How many people can stream your app at once, where they connect from, and what happens when either limit is reached.

Concurrent users

CCU is how many people are streaming at the same moment — not how many visit in a day.

The distinction that matters

If 100 people use your app across a day but never more than 6 are streaming at once, you are using 6 CCU. Capacity is about the peak, not the total.

A session ending releases its CCU immediately, and the next person waiting can use it. So the practical questions are how many people arrive together, and how long each one stays — which is why the idle timeout matters more than it sounds: a session nobody is using still occupies a slot somebody else wants.

Regions

PlanWhere your app can run

EnterpriseGlobal — deployment in your preferred cloud region

Viewers are served from the nearest available region. When that region has no capacity, the session is routed to another one rather than refused — the stream works, and it works with more latency.

This is worth knowing before an event rather than during one. A demo in Asia on a Core plan is served from the USA or Europe, and the distance is felt as lag no amount of tuning removes. If your audience is far from both, a dedicated VM in their region is the answer, and it needs arranging in advance.

You can pin a region for a configuration in the developer options, and confirm which one actually served a session from the Info button inside the stream.

Upload speed by region

How quickly your build reaches our machines depends on where you are uploading from. It affects how long an upload takes and nothing about how the app streams afterwards — a slow upload from a distant office does not produce a slow stream.
